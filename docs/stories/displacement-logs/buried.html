📡 PACKET TX-225-004 — KHETEF'S TOMB

Local: c. 2055–1650 BC | Signal Integrity: 33%

The team arrives on high ground above a river valley dominated by pyramids. Investigator 89 now believes the detector and the signal source together open a temporary passage between eras. His working theory: the detector is built to find and interface with Resonance Anchors hidden in each era. Contact opens the way to what he calls "the next simulation," and without it there is no way out. From the clothing and how weathered the pyramids are, Investigator 28 places them in the Middle Kingdom. The detector points west, past the city and into the desert.

With no money and clothes that mark them as foreign, the team draws stares in the town below. Only Investigator 07 has any chance of blending in. One onlooker hurries off as if to warn someone. In the market, Investigator 66 approaches an elderly merchant with open hands, and Investigator 42 follows. The woman takes to Investigator 42 like a grandmother finding a parched grandchild. She shares water and what little food she has. Her home has two altars: Investigator 89 identifies one to Dagon, a god of grain, and one to an aspect of Baal associated with recompense.

An old man who seems about to report them changes his mind when Investigator 66 offers him a few coins. He leads the team to a room hidden under his floor, beneath a reed mat and a plank door, and drags a table over the hatch. Minutes later the team hears hushed, angry voices overhead and the old man being roughed up. He never comes back for them. They stay hidden through the night. At dawn Investigator 28 slips out to scout. The merchant has a welt across her face, and the old man is already working in the fields.

The team scrapes together cloth for sun cover and containers for water. To leave no trail, they cross the open desert directly, about ten hours on foot, and all arrive intact at a cave mouth beside a 40-foot drop. A structure juts out over the ledge, and the detector points through its doorway.

PERSONAL LOG FRAGMENT — Investigator 89:
"The inscription doesn't end where every transcription says it does. I copied the damaged versions off a projector screen at Brown years ago, after a professor told me, 'Ancient priests weren't designing ciphers, Mr. ▓▓▓▓▓. They were writing theology.' I carried that notebook everywhere. The missing glyphs were never destroyed or mistranslated. Nobody had stood here to record them. I was right."
Recorded: tomb antechamber, local time unknown

Investigator 89 makes a pencil rubbing of the full funerary cipher. Investigator 28 opens the inner door onto a large chamber with a mosaic floor and a ledge, and a second door above it. The floor design suggests a sacrificial pit under the center, so the team keeps to the edges as they move deeper into what is clearly a burial complex: the tomb of Khetef. Investigator 89 notes that the detector's signal has been weakening since they arrived in this era.

🗂️ BUREAU ANALYST NOTES

Packet TX-225-005 is almost entirely lost. The Bureau does not know what happened in the rest of Khetef's tomb, what served as this era's anchor, or how the team got out. The next readable packet places them in Imperial Rome.

Two things concern analysts. First, the detector's signal weakened steadily during this era; if that continues, the team's time in each era may be limited. Second, Investigator 89 calls each era a "simulation." Whether that is a metaphor or a literal finding is not yet known.
